# AFS PTO Policy

## Definitions and Scope

The key terms used in this policy are defined as follows:

- **Vacation / Vacation Time:** Annual time away from work that an employee may use.

- **Vacation Pay:** Amounts accrued under applicable employment standards legislation and company policy for payment to an employee when vacation is taken. Vacation Time and Vacation Pay are related but separate concepts.

- **Accrued Vacation:** Vacation Time accumulated and displayed in the company's internal vacation management system based on an employee's length of service. It is an administrative balance and does not change when statutory Vacation Entitlement arises or when it must be used.

- **Vacation Earning Year:** The consecutive twelve-month period measured from an employee's hire date or Employment Anniversary Date. Statutory Vacation Entitlement is determined by this period.

- **Calendar Year:** January 1 through December 31. Sick Leave is administered on a Calendar Year basis under this policy.

- **Vacation Use Period:** The period during which statutory Vacation Time earned during a Vacation Earning Year must be used. For employees in British Columbia, this is generally the twelve months following the end of that Vacation Earning Year.

- **Advance Vacation:** Vacation Time that AFS approves, at an employee's written request, before the related statutory Vacation Entitlement has arisen. Where permitted by law, Advance Vacation may be deducted from the related entitlement when it arises.

- **Probationary Period:** The initial probationary period stated in the employee's employment agreement.

- **Sick Leave:** Leave used when an employee cannot work because of their own illness, injury, or other health-related reason.

- **Paid Leave:** Leave for which wages are paid under company policy and applicable law.

- **Unpaid Leave / Unpaid Time:** Leave or absence time for which wages are not paid.

***This policy describes AFS's internal PTO and attendance standards. It does not limit or replace minimum rights under applicable provincial employment standards legislation. If company policy conflicts with applicable law, the statutory minimum standard applies.***

## 1. Vacation

### 1.1 Vacation Accrual and Use

- Vacation Pay begins accruing on an employee's first day of employment.

- Vacation accrues during the Probationary Period.

- Vacation Pay accrues at **4% of Eligible Earnings**.
	* Vacation Pay is an accrual of money and is administered separately from Vacation Time.
	- For a typical AFS employee working five days per week, two weeks of annual Vacation Time is administratively managed as **10 vacation days**.

- For administrative purposes, 10 annual vacation days may be displayed as approximately **0.83 days per month** *(10 ÷ 12 = 0.8333 days per month)*.

- The 0.83-day monthly display is an internal administrative method, not a statutory accrual rate under British Columbia employment standards legislation. Monthly amounts do not each have separate expiry dates; they are managed together within the employee's Vacation Year.

- Statutory annual vacation under British Columbia employment standards generally arises after twelve consecutive months of employment. For employee convenience, AFS may allow an employee who has successfully completed probation to use Vacation Time shown as accrued in the company system before completing one year of service.

- Vacation used before one year of service is treated as Advance Vacation and is deducted from the entitlement for that Vacation Year when it arises.

- After completing probation, an employee may request Vacation Time within the amount shown as accrued in the system. Vacation use requires manager or executive approval.

- Advance Vacation requests must be made through the HR Portal, email, or another written method recognized by AFS.

- Vacation continues to accrue during probation, but paid vacation generally may not be used during probation unless separately approved by a manager or executive.

- Requests for one or more vacation days should be made at least **one month in advance** whenever possible.

- Vacation longer than one week must be requested at least one month in advance and requires final executive approval.

**Note:** Under British Columbia employment standards, employees employed for **five days or less are not entitled to annual Vacation Pay**.

***AFS's Vacation Time and Vacation Pay are administered at or above the applicable statutory minimum. If the statutory minimum changes or a higher statutory entitlement applies based on length of service, the applicable statutory standard will be followed.***

### 1.2 Vacation Pay Payout

- An employee may request payment of Vacation Pay that has already accrued without taking vacation.
	*This does not mean that future Vacation Time or future Vacation Pay may be paid in advance.*

- At an employee's request, accrued Vacation Pay may be paid through the company's payroll process and in accordance with applicable employment standards legislation.

- Payment of Vacation Pay does not eliminate or limit statutory Vacation Time to which the employee is entitled.

#### 1.2.1 Termination of Employment

When employment ends, all unpaid Vacation Pay that must be paid under applicable law and the company's payroll records will be paid, regardless of length of service or probationary status.

#### 1.2.2 Unused Vacation and Use Period

- Statutory Vacation Time earned during a Vacation Year must be used within the applicable statutory period after that Vacation Year ends.

- For British Columbia employees, Vacation Time earned during a Vacation Year is generally available during the following twelve months.

- Statutory unused Vacation Time is not subject to a five-day carryover limit and does not automatically expire or decrease on the Employment Anniversary Date.

- When vacation is used, the existing Vacation Time with the earliest use deadline is used first.

- If Vacation Time remains unused as its use deadline approaches, the employee and manager or HR should coordinate scheduling so it can be used within the applicable period.

- Where necessary, AFS may request or schedule vacation after considering applicable employment standards legislation and operational requirements.

**Example:** An employee hired on October 1, 2025, with 10 annual vacation days who uses one day during the first year:

>October 1, 2025 - September 30, 2026: first Vacation Year
>	Total Vacation for the first Vacation Year: **10 days**
>	Used during the first year: 1 day
> 
> Balance as of October 1, 2026: 9 days
>	The remaining 9 days should generally be used by *September 30, 2027*
>	Vacation for the second Vacation Year begins accruing at the same time on October 1, 2026

***If AFS provides Vacation Time above the statutory minimum, AFS may establish a separate carryover standard for the excess portion.***

## 2. Sick Leave

### 2.1 Applicable Jurisdiction

Sick Leave eligibility is determined under the employment standards legislation applicable to the employee. Employees working outside British Columbia may therefore have different Sick Leave entitlements under the law applicable to them.

### 2.2 British Columbia Employees

An employee who has completed **90 consecutive days of employment** may use the following Sick Leave under British Columbia employment standards:

#### Paid Sick Leave

- **5 paid Sick Leave days** are available in each Calendar Year.

#### Unpaid Sick Leave

- Up to **3 unpaid Sick Leave days** are available in each Calendar Year.

#### Reset Date

- BC Sick Leave resets each year on **January 1**.

<mark style="background:rgba(240, 200, 0, 0.2)">- Vacation is administered by Vacation Year, based on the employee's hire date, while Sick Leave is administered by Calendar Year, from January 1 through December 31.</mark>

#### Carryover

- Paid and unpaid Sick Leave do not carry into the next Calendar Year.
- **Unused Sick Leave has no monetary value and is not paid out in cash.**

## 3. Medical Documentation

HR may request **reasonably sufficient proof for Sick Leave** or other health-related absences to the extent permitted by applicable employment standards legislation.

Situations that may be reviewed include:
- repeated or excessive Sick Leave
- frequent absences
- the length or circumstances of an absence
- or other situations where HR reasonably determines that documentation is necessary under the law

AFS will not require a doctor's note or note from another health professional where applicable law prohibits that request. Under general BC standards, a health-professional note will generally not be required for the first two occurrences of a short-term health-related absence of five days or less in a Calendar Year, subject to legal exceptions.

Where documentation is needed, AFS will request information that is reasonable and proportionate to the duration, frequency, and circumstances of the absence.

Responsibility for any document-related cost will depend on the document requested, the purpose of the request, and applicable law.

## 4. Excessive Absence

### 4.1 Misuse of Sick Leave

Sick Leave is provided for an employee's own illness, injury, or other health-related absence. It may not be substituted for Vacation or other Personal Leave.

Employees should notify their manager or HR in advance whenever possible when a scheduled medical appointment, treatment, or similar health-related absence is required. For unexpected Sick Leave, employees must notify their manager or HR as soon as reasonably possible.

If a reasonable concern arises about Sick Leave use or overall attendance, HR may review attendance records, assess the circumstances, and request reasonably necessary documentation to the extent permitted by law.

### 4.2 Attendance Policy Violations and Discipline

Repeated or excessive unauthorized absenteeism, misuse of leave, or failure to follow company attendance and absence-notification procedures may result in discipline under applicable employment standards and human rights legislation, up to and including termination in serious cases.

***Approved leave and leave protected by applicable law are not attendance misconduct.***

## 5. Leave Units

Vacation may be requested in half-day or full-day units subject to manager or executive approval.

Employees may also request multiple working days or one or more consecutive weeks of Vacation.

These units do not limit statutory annual vacation rights. 

### 5.1 Minimum Leave Unit

- Half day (AM or PM)
- Full day

### 5.2 Hourly Vacation

Hourly Vacation, including one-, two-, or three-hour Vacation, may be used when separately approved in advance by a manager or executive.

### 5.3 Absences Shorter Than Half a Day

An absence shorter than a half day is ***generally treated as Unpaid Time*** unless separately approved by a manager or executive.

## 6. Lunch Break

### 6.1 Unpaid Lunch Break

Employees receive a **30-minute unpaid lunch break**.

Employees must be completely relieved from work during the unpaid lunch break. If AFS requires an employee to work or remain available during the lunch break, that time will be treated as working time in accordance with applicable employment standards legislation.

### 6.2 Restrictions

The unpaid lunch break may not be used to offset **lateness or to leave work early**.

**Example:** For working hours of 8:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. with a lunch break from 12:00 p.m. to 12:30 p.m., the following are not permitted:

- Starting at 8:30 a.m. and skipping the lunch break
- Leaving at 4:00 p.m. and using the lunch break to make up the time
